Causal Models with Tiny Data: Rural People Living with Dementia

Ranveer Singh, Saurabh Mathur, Sriraam Natarajan, and collaborators · AIME 2026

Life-space mobility — a person's physical and social environment, movement, and daily activities — declines with cognitive decline in older adults, but assessments have mostly focused on urban populations, leaving rural and Indigenous communities underserved.

With only 20 patients from rural and Indigenous communities in Minnesota and Ontario, we compared four ways of building a causal graph: expert elicitation, LLM consensus (Claude, Gemini, GPT), hybrid subtractive refinement, and a data-driven FCI baseline. Experts and LLMs agreed on most edges but inverted one key relationship — whether caregiver burden or life-space score is the ultimate outcome — revealing a real difference in what each treats as central to dementia care. None of the graphs fit the data well, but they failed in different, informative ways.
