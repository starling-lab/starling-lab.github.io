Knowledge-based Learning

Phillip Odom, Shuo Yang, Mayukh Das, Harsha Kokel, Saurabh Mathur

Knowledge-rich approaches to learning and sequential decision-making try to counter the noise, stochasticity, and asymmetry of knowledge that plague purely data-driven techniques, by leveraging expertise accumulated by domain experts over years of experience.

Our group builds knowledge-augmented frameworks for representing and eliciting knowledge from one or many experts, to jointly learn the structure and parameters of compact models — for prediction and regression tasks as well as sequential decision-making such as reinforcement learning, planning, and imitation learning. A particular focus is "asymmetry of knowledge": an AI agent may have ample compute but lack the implicit priorities a human expert understands intuitively. More recently we've extended this to knowledge-guided deep models, addressing one of deep learning's core bottlenecks — sub-optimal learning under sparse or noisy data.
